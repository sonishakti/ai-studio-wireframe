### Directions
1. Emphasis rail (within DS) — a 2px accent rail and filled icon roundel mark the selected card only; the other two stay quieter than today.
2. Compact chip row (stretch) — the three stacked cards become one horizontal row of icon chips so the whole decision fits with zero scroll.
3. Two-column definition (within DS) — Name plus an outcome-preview chip sit in a left column, the three type cards sit in a right column.
4. Confidence footer (within DS) — the selected card grows an inline footer naming the exact save outcome; the sheet-level reason line is removed and moves into the card.
5. Editorial single column (stretch) — the helper sentence becomes the section's real heading, cards get more room, and the sheet reads as two clear beats: name, then reach.

### Critique summary
Evaluated against hierarchy, emphasis-by-exception, DS-conformance and scan speed:
1. **Emphasis rail** — strongest on pop-out/emphasis: a single accent rail plus filled roundel reads before color does, and quieting the other two cards resolves the three-way visual tie the current screen has. Risk: on light theme the rail can read as a stray border if the accent token sits too close to the divider color.
2. **Compact chip row** — best for zero-scroll and groups the decision into one gesture, but collapsing stacked cards into chips drops the one-line copy that currently disambiguates batch vs code; needs a tooltip or footer to recover it.
3. **Two-column definition** — a clean left-identity/right-decision split that matches how Studio already separates identity from configuration elsewhere, but on narrow viewports the columns stack and the outcome-preview chip lands under the fold, working against confirm-before-scroll.
4. **Confidence footer** — most reassurance-forward: naming the exact save outcome inline removes a re-read of the sheet-level reason line (redundancy reduction) and stays DS-safe by reusing existing card/footer tokens. Main cost: only the selected card grows, so selection changes can cause a small layout jump.
5. **Editorial single column** — reads best on a first open (larger targets, the sentence-as-heading aids scanning) but costs vertical space and is the furthest from current density; flagged as stretch and needs a scroll-fold check at 375px.

**Net**: directions 1 and 4 are safest to prototype first (within DS, low layout risk); 2 and 3 trade information density for compactness and need a fallback for the dropped copy / narrow viewport; 5 is the highest-risk, highest-clarity stretch option.

### Directions
1. Contrast-weighted cards (within DS) — the selected card carries the weight with a stronger border and fill; the other two keep full-contrast titles and one quieter step for their stack. References: 07 Resend plans modal (weight, not colour), 08 Zapier pricing tabs (quiet logos).
2. Stack-as-hero row (within DS) — presets shrink to a name and one line; the Listen, Think and Speak rows carry the logo, the model and their own Language and Voice choice. References: 05 Hume AI EVI playground, 01 ElevenLabs settings panel. Logos: Deepgram, Google, OpenAI; Cartesia keeps the C mark.
3. Single-column focus card (within DS) — one ToggleGroup names each preset and its trade-off; the chosen preset's stack, Language, Voice and region line sit in one column below. References: 03 Cartesia numbered sections, 08 Zapier tab selector, 06 Delphi settings card.
4. Voice spotlight (stretch, within DS) — the voice comes first: one card with play, name and Change; the three presets shrink to a name, one line and a logo strip that lines up across cards. References: 05 Hume AI EVI voice card, 02 ElevenLabs voice library, Retell agent editor, ChatGPT voice picker (Refero shot 3c237d7b) for the open list.
5. Only what changes (stretch) — a compact radio row above one Listen, Think and Speak strip; the swapped tile takes the weight, and Language and Voice live in their tiles. The connector treatment is the stretch part. References: Vapi assistant header, 05 Hume AI, 07 Resend, 11 Linear, fal.ai workflow nodes (Refero shot 625f6cc2).

### Critique summary
Evaluated against hierarchy, emphasis-by-exception, DS-conformance and scan speed, against the V0 Current baseline:
1. **Contrast-weighted cards** — closest 1:1 swap for today's row: same 3-card grid, same components, only the weight token changes, so it is the safest to prototype first. Risk: on light theme the stronger border can sit close to the divider colour and needs a contrast check.
2. **Stack-as-hero row** — best at making the actual differentiator do the work: real vendor logos replace the two-letter monograms and each module gets its own model line, so Sam reads what's running without opening Custom. Risk: three full stack rows plus per-preset selects add height inside the same row envelope.
3. **Single-column focus card** — best scan speed for the chosen preset (nothing competing for attention), and reuses the existing Overview/Agent/Runs tab pattern so it stays DS-safe. Risk: hides the other two presets' copy behind a switch, working against compare-before-commit.
4. **Voice spotlight** — reframes the hero around the outcome Sam actually judges (does it sound right) by leading with a playable voice card before the presets; grounded in Hume AI's and ElevenLabs' voice-first panels via Refero. Risk: demotes the preset decision itself to second billing, which is the job step's actual point.
5. **Only what changes** — most compact: one radio row plus a single Listen/Think/Speak strip removes the three-times-repeated stack entirely; Refero-grounded against Vapi, Resend and Linear. Best fit for a dense, technical read. Risk: the swapped-tile connector treatment is not yet a token in `DESIGN.md`.

**Net**: variation 1 is the safest first prototype (zero new surface, fully DS-conformant); 2 and 3 are the next-safest within-DS options, trading stack visibility (2) against single-preset focus (3); 4 and 5 are the Refero-grounded stretches — 4 is the stronger pick if Sam's real blocker is voice fit rather than preset choice, 5 if the goal is maximum density.
