A6 — Concurrent lines: self-serve capacity, bought where it hurts

The Billing “Concurrent lines” card + the wizard’s at-the-wall unlock. Lines govern how many calls run at once; the spend cap governs dollars — and the design says so, because no competitor does.

● Shipped 2026-07-09 commit 3aeccd6 Live: /billing ↗ PRD: roadmap-features-prd §3 · A6

Why this feature exists

Self-serve concurrency is table stakes (5/5 competitors sell it without a sales call) and it sits directly on the Convert transition: the moment a batch operator needs more lines is the moment of expansion intent. Research found the market splits into line-item add-ons (Vapi $10/line, Retell $8/slot + burst) and plan-gating (ElevenLabs, Bland) — and exposed three genuine gaps nobody ships: an in-product purchase moment at the wall, an explicit reconciliation with spend limits, and a documented downgrade path. A6 closes all three.

Diverge → converge

A · Line-item stepper card

Billing card + sheet, Vapi-style. Best IA fit and X1 idiom match; best peer-choice (“Keep queuing”). Weakness: the wall lived only on Billing.

B · Limits panel + live gauge

Quotas-tab panel, Retell-style. Cleanest math (zero indefensible figures) but sell-first ordering, no decline affordance, buried placement.

C · At-the-wall-first

Embeddable moment where the limit hurts — the whitespace itself. Won conversion; lost money-honesty on hardcoded cycle days and an over-claiming button.

Verdict: A wins on the highest cross-lens floor — its two disqualifying-tier defects were repairable by edit, C’s money violations were structural, B’s IA violations architectural. Shipped = A’s chassis + C’s wall placement (re-implemented inside OutboundSettings) and show-your-work kit + B’s live-recomputed math and precisely-scoped cap claim. All 12 judge fixes applied — including the deepest one: with the seeded data, the spend cap would pause calls before the promised speed-up pays off, so the sheet now says exactly that and points at the cap review.

What shipped

Billing · concurrency-card.tsx

  • Live gauge (role="meter") with the included/purchased seam tick
  • Wall = primary-tone information: “new batch calls queue — nothing drops”; “Keep queuing” is a first-class choice
  • Time-saved estimate shows its inputs: queue × ~2 min ÷ lines, Estimate badge
  • AddLinesSheet: quick chips, exact commit amounts (“Add 5 lines · $28.39 today”), proration from the period position, reduce-anytime with prorated credit
  • Cap separation stated: “line fees bill separately — your cap governs per-minute spend only”; warns when the cap bites before the speed-up pays off

The wall, where it hurts

  • Wizard batch step: choosing a max-concurrent above capacity surfaces the inline capacity note + Add lines (one component owns ALL capacity communication — a split select-suffix + note was rejected as drift)
  • CONCURRENCY + concurrencyStats() in the lib; PAYG_RATE hoisted — one rate constant product-wide
  • “Concurrent channels” quota renamed “RTC concurrent channels” — a different product’s quota, no more collision
  • Events: concurrency_wall_viewed · lines_added · keep_queuing_clicked (the decline is data too)
  • Monitor’s wall banner deferred to D1 — it extracts this card’s banner as the shared moment

Measurement

Conversion: concurrency_wall_viewed → lines_added rate (the whitespace hypothesis: wall-placed purchase converts better than billing-page discovery). Counter-metrics: keep_queuing_clicked (a healthy decline rate proves no dark pattern) and post-purchase spend_cap_hit within the hour (lines bought into a cap wall = the reconciliation failed).
