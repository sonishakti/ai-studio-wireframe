D1 — “Paced ≠ failed”: the batch throttling view

A real detail page for a live outbound batch, built around one idea no competitor states out loud: a batch dialing at its capacity ceiling is working, not broken — and the design makes that gut-read instant, while a genuinely degraded batch reads as unmistakably needing attention.

The failure mode this prevents

Research across Retell, Vapi, Bland, and Twilio found the same gap: platforms show a flat “Sent” counter or a spinner, and every unexplained stall reads as broken. Twilio’s own high-volume guidance is “queue-and-retry, not drop,” but no product says “you’re paced, and that’s fine” to the operator. So a campaign quietly throttled by its concurrency ceiling looks identical to one that’s failing — and the operator panics, pauses it, or files a ticket. D1 replaces the redirect-to-Monitor with a page whose whole job is to answer “is this working?” before any telemetry.

Paced (working)“Working — dialing at capacity. 214 queued, none dropped. ~14 min to finish.” Primary tone, never amber. The A6 Add lines unlock sits right here.
Degraded (act)“Auto-paused — this batch needs attention.” Carrier failures hit 22% (SIP 503), so the circuit breaker stopped it. Destructive tone · Check the trunk · Resume anyway.

The batch-health state model

Deployment pacing: scheduled → dialing → paced → draining → done, with paused (user) and degraded (circuit-breaker) as off-ramps. The key semantic: paced is primary/working; degraded is destructive. Per-call dispositions carry the real telephony taxonomy — completed · no-answer · busy · voicemail · disconnected · wrong-number · carrier-failed · retrying · max-retries — where busy/no-answer re-enter the retry cadence and disconnected/wrong-number are flagged and skipped (retrying a dead number is waste, not pacing).

Diverge → converge (3 variants)

1 · Ops console

Dense telemetry. Won operator-answers (8.8) — most complete picture — but never states the “working” verdict in words.

2 · Reassurance-first

One leading StateBanner = the verdict, flipped primary↔destructive. Won is-this-working (8.8) + fold-fit (8.4).

3 · Timeline / flow

Pipeline nodes make “queued, not dropped” spatial. Strongest degraded read; but red disposition nodes always show in the paced flow.

Verdict: V2 wins the load-bearing lens (the verdict-in-one-sentence is the most legible paced/degraded contrast) + fold-fit; V3 was disqualified on that lens because its outcomes fan always renders red nodes inside the paced flow, letting the calm case read broken. Shipped = V2’s reassurance chassis + V1’s complete telemetry (dedicated concurrency meter, precise “92s longest wait”, page chrome) with all 6 judge fixes: disposition breakdown expanded on live batches, bars scaled to total, max-retries surfaced, and the amber/red split resolved by making the auto-pause a true degraded pacing state.

Fold-in & parity

Route + data

  • Replaces the redirect at /deploy/batch-calls/[id] with a real page wired to getDeployment(id)
  • Deployment.batchRuntime — pacing, dispositions, retry, cps, queue depth + reason
  • batchEta() = live pace × remaining queue (no competitor exposes this)
  • Reuses shipped idioms: StateBanner (X1), AddLinesSheet (A6), HealthDot

Monitor parity + hooks

  • deploymentHealth(): degraded → unhealthy dot, so the Monitor list and the detail banner agree; paced stays healthy
  • The degraded StateBanner is the single insertion point for D6 Alerting — same signal feeds Monitor
  • The X1 spend-cap headroom threads into the A6 unlock at the wall — the three features compose
