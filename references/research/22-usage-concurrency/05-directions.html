What the audit says out loud

A fails. One row out of thirty. It happens to be the row the tracker's JTBD sentence is written on, and it is the row we are not allowed to answer yet. Everything else A touches it touches by accident, because fixing three nulls is not the same as fixing the surface they were passed to. A direction that only answers the happy path fails the audit, and A answers the happy path by charging for it.

E fails, and it fails on the honesty floor rather than on the count. A zero column usually means a weak direction. Here it means the direction is a finance proposal with a card drawn around it.

C and D together answer 21 of 30 rows fully and 6 in part, and they do not overlap once. That is not a coincidence and it is not two features: C is the acting half of the JTBD and D is the understanding half, and the JTBD's own happy path ends at step 8, on D's screen, not at step 7 on C's.

Rainy 14, 20 and 21 have no Y in any column, and should not have one. 14 is a checkout processor, and no direction that survives has a checkout. 20 is the vendor failing mid-call: the audio task bills per minute with no exclusion for a failed component, so drawing a credit would invent a refund we do not give. 21 is the caller hanging up while the meter runs: minutes are stop_ts - start_ts and nothing in the contract ties the agent's stop to the caller's, so the only honest surface for it is a bug report against Engine, not a control.

Rainy 24 is the one C cannot close. A request has a state, a number and a home on the surface that caused it, which beats a support link in a different tool, but it is still a human on the other end. It closes when owner question 2 is answered, and not before.

Verdict

C, with D as the second half rather than a graft. One capacity object read by every mount, and a usage page whose finest grain is an agent and a model, in minutes.

The reason is one sentence that the shipped sheet almost says and no competitor says at all: lines change how fast you spend, not how much. Clearing 186 queued calls costs about 372 agent minutes at 15 lines and about 372 agent minutes at 20. The shipped warning compares cap headroom against minutes saved, which is the weaker half of that idea; the strong half is that the money is fixed and only the clock moves, so the control the account actually needs at the wall is often the cap, not the lines. Once that is the sentence, the purchase path stops being the point of the feature, which is exactly the position the lock leaves us in.

What I am not doing, and why. Not selling lines. The price is a placeholder the code admits to and the ceiling is a flag Engine cannot set, so the sheet asks instead of charging, and the commit says Agora replies by email rather than naming an amount. Not drawing credits. There is no credits object in either codebase or on docs.agora.io, the account has a balance and two entitlements, and the fix for "I cannot tell whether credits and balance are the same money" is to stop saying credits. Not putting money beside a model. Agora charges $0.10 a minute whichever model runs, a Cost tab built on vendor list prices was deleted after audit on 2026-05-26, and the mode column plus a minutes column is the version that is both useful and true. Not building Archer. It appears three times in this repo, all of them the same roadmap row, and nowhere in ng-console or on docs.agora.io. 868khqb6d is a P0 due this month and it has no surface here, on purpose, until somebody says what it is.

The open question, not designable without an answer. Is capacity sold, requested, or sales-assisted? The prototype ships the request, because it is the only one of the three that can be built without a price finance has set and it is the only one that makes the ceiling visible, which nothing does today. If the answer is "sell it", the sheet's footer gains a prorated amount and lines_added starts firing, and every other line on the screen stays as drawn. If the answer is "sales-assisted", the request becomes a mailto and the sheet loses its number input. The screen is built so that either answer is a footer change.

Requires Engine, each one named on screen

  1. A settable concurrency limit. The contract exposes no field to read or set, only ConcurrencyLimitExceeded on a 422 alongside ResourceQuotaLimitExceeded and AccountSuspended. On screen: the sheet's footer caption and the commit that promises an email, not a change.
  2. A readable ceiling. The six fields to file against Engine are Retell's, verbatim: current_concurrency, base_concurrency, purchased_concurrency, concurrency_limit, concurrency_purchase_limit, remaining_purchase_limit. Until they exist the gauge reads the Console's own feature flag. On screen: "Agora sets that number."
  3. Minutes attributed per agent and per model. There is no usage object in the agent API; minutes are stop_ts - start_ts and pipeline_id is the only join back to a Studio agent. The live catalog's convoai-agent-call-minutes row carries metrics: []. On screen: the breakdown's estimate disclosure.
  4. A capacity price and a purchase path. Deliberately not built, and no per-line price exists that finance has set. On screen: the request path, which names no amount.
  5. Multimodal models on a managed key. Verbatim from the docs: no MLLM vendor-model is available under an Agora Managed Key, and the Console enforces it in one line. On screen: the mode column's MLLM state.
  6. Managed-mode billing. The PRD's own blocking question: postpaid or a new SKU. It decides whether managed and BYO produce different invoice lines. On screen: the mode column carries no money at all, which is the only version that survives either answer.
  7. Archer. Not an Engine gap, a definition gap. Nothing is drawn for 868khqb6d, and the build must not invent a surface for it.

Audit corrections

Three hostile audits read this verdict, on locks, on honesty and on buildability. All three refuted it. What follows is what they killed, and what was kept against them. The build plan is the corrected one, not the one drawn above.

  1. The capacity request, its pending state and its reply channel. requestCapacity(), clearCapacityRequest(), the above_published_ceiling refusal and the line "Requested 20 lines on Sep 17. Agora replies by email." No request endpoint exists, no ticket id comes back, and nothing published says 20 is the most Agora will grant. A stored "pending" chip asserts that Agora received something. The escalation now routes to /help/contact, which is a real surface in this app and is the Console's own answer at ng-console i18n :4207-4209. It also stops the verdict from silently answering owner question 2, which the brief's partial lock reserves.
  2. Two entitlements on one number line. base: 15 came from DEFAULT_CAMPAIGN_CONCURRENCY_MAX, the fallback ceiling on one campaign's concurrency field, granted per customer through a feature flag. PUBLISHED_PCU_CEILING = 20 is Engine's peak concurrent users per App ID. An account-wide gauge reading 15 of 15 with a quick pick asking support for 20 draws a value neither source can produce. One number survives: 20, the only one Agora publishes. base, granted and source are gone with it, and so are "granted to this project" and "Agora sets that number".
  3. queued: 186, and the money hung off it. 186 is not computable from cmp_q2_west, which carries 248 contacts and no dialed counter. The only queue depths in the data are batchRuntime.queued. The card now sums the dialing batches, which is 214, one queue with one source.
  4. AVG_CALL_MIN, and every figure downstream of it. "about 372 agent minutes" and "$37.20" are 186 times an unmeasured 2, times the rate. The house derivation for this exact question is batchEta(), on observed pace, locked at LEARNINGS :519 rule 5. Promoting the guess into a lib module and multiplying it into a dollar amount put two clocks on one queue. The wall estimate is now batchEta(), the same clock the batch page prints.
  5. The fifth mount. BatchRuntime carries its own linesInUse and linesTotal, all 10, and its reason string renders verbatim. "One capacity object every mount reads" left the batch page saying 10 lines while billing said 15. linesTotal is deleted and the batch page reads the store; the reason at :1449 loses its line count.
  6. The seed that could not count. readCapacity({ inUse: calls.length }) made /monitor/live say "5 of 15 in use right now" while /billing said "15 of 15". inUse is now composed from the parts that exist: five live calls plus the dialing batch's lines. Raising that batch to 15 puts the account at 20 of 20, so the wall is reachable without a fake ceiling.
  7. The MANAGED_PROVIDERS edit and the mode column that forced it. The list is Record<string, number> and the number prints as a bring-your-own per-minute rate; ARES has no key to bring and no rate any source carries. Dropping ElevenLabs without migrating vc_02 leaves a managed row for a vendor the catalog no longer resells, two surfaces disagreeing. Three consumers read the list, two of them feature 07's.   8. "exhausted_no_card". PLAN_USAGE ships 150 of 300 free minutes, so the new member is unreachable, which is the same charge the verdict laid against CONCURRENCY. It is filed with the fixture move that makes it reachable, and the branches it forces are written in that pass.

Mounted in the dashboard layout it would have raced the wizard's own focusOn on every builder route, two smooth scrolls and two outlines over one anchor, and useSearchParams there would have de-opted every dashboard route from static prerender.   10. Four doors onto one action. The store fixed the arithmetic and left four front doors. Owner question 7 is answered the way "one door per action" answers it: the sheet has one mount, and monitor, batch and the wizard link to /billing?focus=concurrent-lines. That deletes the capHeadroomUsd={null} problem rather than routing around it, and it deletes the undefined prop contract the third audit found.
  11. Two claimed facts that do not hold. copy-lint.mjs collects .tsx only, so every .ts file is already outside it and moving sentences into lib/billing-state.ts buys no coverage. And the cap warning is dead in four doors, not three: two hardcode null, the other two compute it from a spendCapUsd that is null. Neither claim is used as a reason any more.

Kept against the audits

  1. The spine, C with D as its second half. One capacity object every surface reads, and a usage page whose finest grain is an agent in minutes. Both audits that attacked the plan attacked its values, not its shape, and the third confirmed every other line citation.
  2. The sentence, and the sheet that carries it. "Lines change how fast you spend, not how much" needs no average call length: it follows from the flat $0.10 rate. The half that needed an assumption is cut; the half that does not is arithmetic on the free minutes left and the line count. The first audit would have deleted the sheet with the request. It stays, because it is where the only idea no competitor ships now lives, and because it is the footer the owner's answer changes.
  3. The A6 rule sentence at concurrency-card.tsx :190-193. Learning 3 says it keeps its sentence and the first audit was right to say the plan deleted the line it named. It keeps the rule and loses the price token: "At capacity, batch calls queue. Nothing drops. Your spend cap governs per-minute spend."
  4. "Keep queuing" as a first-class, tracked decline. Untouched, and now reachable, which it was not.
  5. The shared money overlay. The one genuine write path: a card added in the spend card has to move the budget line in the concurrency card directly below it. This is what lib/billing-state.ts is for now that capacity is read-only.
  6. Deleting the Pro tier. A 10,000-minute allowance sold for $99 is $1,000 of minutes at the published rate. No audit contested it.
