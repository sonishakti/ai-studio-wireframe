Phone Number Purchase Directions

JTBD. When my agent already answers me in the browser and the only thing between it and a real customer is a number they can dial, I want to get one and hear it ring on my own phone in the same sitting.

Where it landsThe number field in the builder's Deployment section (PhoneNumberSelect, channel-section.tsx:298-355), the sheet its footer opens (add-phone-number-sheet.tsx), and the inventory row (deploy/phone-numbers/page.tsx).
Agora factsEngine is $0.10 per agent minute, the same with your own key. Telephony is Beta and free. A rented number is the platform's first recurring per-resource fee; no meter, plan card or spend cap models one.
Contractprovider is a closed byo | twilio. The add payload is seven fields: no country, price, capability, status, owner or identity record. delete removes the config, not the number.
LockedOne door per action. Third branch of the existing sheet, never a fourth door. Included and purchased stay separate numbers. Commit buttons carry exact amounts. The flow ends on a placed call.

Five directions. Each is a different answer to one question: where does the decision live, and who holds the wait between paying and ringing? Everything below the fold of that question (which area code field, which price format) follows from it.

A · A Buy a number page

The obvious one. A new route under Deploy with search, results, checkout and an order history.

Give purchasing its own address, /deploy/phone-numbers/buy, reached from a second primary on the inventory header. Search by country and area code, filter by capability, compare prices in a table, add identity documents, check out. The page has room to grow a country picker, a KYC record, a per-country requirements panel and an order list, all of which Phase 2 will ask for.

This is what a Console does. It is also what a designer reaches for when a ticket says "let customers search available US numbers, see capabilities and pricing, purchase a number".

B · A third branch of the sheet, ending at the receipt

Buy sits beside Quick connect and Manual SIP. Money moves, the sheet closes, the row says Turning up.

The toggle at the top of AddPhoneNumberSheet goes from two to three: Buy · Quick connect · Manual SIP. The buy branch is one field and a result list, because that is all a Phase 1 purchase needs: Vapi asks for an area code, Retell's area_code is "3 digit integer. The number lands in the list with a Turning up badge and the user goes back to what they were doing.

C · The same branch, ending on a ringing phone · recommended core

The sheet holds the wait. The turn-up is a fact log, and the last step is a call the user places.

B's branch, and then the sheet stays open. The wait between "it is mine" and "it works" is the single state nobody in the teardown designs: Vapi warns in prose that "Calls will not work until activation is complete", LiveKit ships a pending status and leaves you to poll it. Here the wait is the same timestamped fact log the quick-connect branch already uses instead of a spinner, and the flow ends the way A3 ends: the user presses Call it now, their own phone rings, the agent speaks.

The identity requirement is stated above the price, not at checkout, because that is the one move no vendor makes: Retell's KYC is found by "click[ing] on any of your numbers", and Twilio's Regulatory Bundle blocks the purchase you already chose. Cheapest differentiator in the feature.

D · The row learns where a number came from, what it costs, and how to give it back

The inventory half. Provenance, a third status, a monthly figure, and two destructive verbs instead of one.

Retell stamps every number retell-twilio | retell-telnyx | custom in the same response that carries the agent binding, so bought and brought numbers live in one list and tell each other apart there. Today our only origin column is Vendor, which prints Bandwidth for a customer's own trunk on the same day Bandwidth becomes the carrier Agora resells through. Vendor becomes From, and an Agora number says Agora.

Status gains Turning up, and a turning-up number stays visible in the number field as a disabled option rather than vanishing from the only place it can be assigned. Release splits in two, because release is a money event and delete is not: End the rental for an Agora number, Remove from Agora for a brought one, each with the sentence that is true for it.

E · Make the first number honest

The lock seam. Aria claims a number the account never acquired, and four surfaces disagree about which number it is.

The ceremony sells the payoff as "no number to buy", the landing then says Aria is live on +1 (628) 555-0188, the inventory says those digits are Sales Inbound on a Twilio trunk behind a different deployment, the sandbox seed is +1 (628) 555-0260, and the constant it claims to match is +1 (415) 555-0100. Four values, one number. The day numbers are purchasable, a new account is told it already has one.

What this direction can decide and what it cannot are different things. Whether first run offers a number, gates a card on it, or grants one free is owner question 1 and it is not designable from here. That the number Aria answers on must be one number, named as a sandbox line, and the same number on every surface that shows it is true under all three answers, costs four seed edits, and stops the product lying before the feature ships.

Three directions fail the audit on their own and the table says which way. A answers the happy path and almost nothing after it: it scores on search, capability and country and then goes blank for eleven consecutive rows, because a buy page has no opinion about a number you already own. B answers the happy path and stops at the receipt: every ◐ in its column is the same defect wearing a different scenario number, a state it creates and then hands to a screen it does not change. D and E fail the top half of the table outright, which is the correct result for a graft and a bug fix. Only C carries the getting-one block, and only D carries the giving-it-back block. Neither is the feature alone.

Rainy 11 is unanswered in every column and stays unanswered. hasBillingAccess requires the FinanceCenter permission in the live Console and there is no permission model in the prototype at all, so a "you cannot buy this, ask your admin" state would be drawn against nothing. It is named in the verdict, not faked.

Verdict

C, with D grafted and E's designable half taken now.

Buy is the third branch of the sheet every number already comes through, it is one field because every vendor's purchase control is one field, and it ends on a call the user places because a number that never rings earns Agora nothing. The row learns three facts it does not have today: where the number came from, what it costs a month, and which of the two destructive verbs applies to it. Aria's number becomes one number on every surface that shows it.

What I am not doing, and why

Not a Buy a number page. A is the direction a lazy reading of the ticket produces, and the ticket's own sentence supports it. It is refused because the number field is where the question is asked and a page answers it two navigations away, and because a bought number and a brought number are one object. When Phase 2 brings a country picker, the branch grows a country field. The room A buys is room for requirements nobody has written yet: 868kj8ux7 and 868kj8u4v are both empty descriptions.

Not a country picker. The two tickets disagree, US/Canada/Peru/Brazil against US/Chile. A country picker for a list nobody has agreed on is a control that would be redrawn the day it is answered. The buy branch states United States as a fact and offers the BYO path for everywhere else, which is true whichever way question 2 falls.

Not verified and branded calling. 868kykk8y is a second object with its own lifecycle, its own per-country enrollment states and its own honesty clause. Drawing it now means shipping a business calling profile before a number model exists to attach it to.

Not the invoice. The commit button carries an exact amount and the end-rental dialog says the final charge shows on the next invoice. Whether the rental is a line beside the $0.10 minutes or its own SKU is 868kj8u4v, and no copy here claims either.

Not a permission state. Rainy 11 is real in the live Console and has nothing to stand on in the prototype. A blocked-buyer screen drawn against no permission model would be theatre.

The open question, which is not designable without an answer

What does the first-run home become once numbers are purchasable? Three answers, three products:

(a) Nothing changes. Aria stays on a sandbox line, the card is still captured at minute 150, and buying a number is a later deliberate act. Cheapest, protects the one thing no competitor does, and leaves the thing that makes an agent real behind a wall the first run never mentions.
(b) The card moves to the number. First run offers Get a number as its second step. Matches four of five competitors and makes the first paid moment concrete, at the price of putting a paywall back into a first run built without one and breaking the half-tier nudge, whose whole premise is a card before exhaustion rather than a card before value.
(c) One free number, then card. Agora grants a number the way it grants Aria. Strongest activation story, the only answer that keeps both, and it spends real carrier money on every signup including the bots.

Blocking on the same answer: billing/plans/page.tsx:48-49 already promises "1 phone number" on Free and "10 phone numbers" on Pro, and nothing grants either. Under (a) and (b) those two strings are a bug and get deleted, which is what the plan below does. Under (c) they become the specification and get written back with a number the product actually hands over. The build does not wait on this: nothing in C or D depends on it, and E's half deliberately stops short of it.

What the contract cannot express, which is Engine work to file now

Six things appear on screen in this design and have nothing to read from. provider is a closed byo | twilio, so the From column cannot say Agora. The list response carries no status, so Turning up cannot be read back. No endpoint returns available numbers, a price or a capability set, so the results list has no source. TelephonyErrorResponse is free text, so the taken-number and provider-error lines have no codes. The add payload has no field for a business name or address, so the identity step has nowhere to write. And nothing anywhere carries a monthly charge, so the Monthly column and the final-charge line are computed by the prototype alone.
